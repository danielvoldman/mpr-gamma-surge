## Estimator caveats due at MS7 (DL-062, DL-073)

Both were left open "to be revisited at MS7 before classifying noisy runs" (DL-062 Status; DL-083 (2)).

**DL-062: the standard errors are too small.** The 2-SE rule uses the plain OLS SE of the slope of ln envelope. The Hilbert envelope of a band-passed signal is smooth and strongly autocorrelated (about 120 to 130 samples per 10 cycles, n_eff far smaller), so the SE is understated and `rate / SE` is inflated. *Effect on D8:* "damped" (rate below -2 SE) is declared too easily. A truly sustained or growing episode with a noisy negative fitted slope can be called damped and so classed NS or D-none, and a noisy envelope in mode N is the worst case. The direction is conservative for a surge claim (fewer surge classes, lower G5 fraction, and an NC1 pass that is partly the estimator's doing, which has to be reported). In S2 the G1 margin is large (rate/SE at least 45, at least 21.6 under the window shifts), so G1 does not depend on it, but the SE values themselves are not trustworthy as written.

**DL-073: step-onset filter bias.** The zero-phase band-pass smears an abrupt onset over about 20 ms and the fit window starts inside that transient. Measured once on a synthetic 40 Hz ring starting at 1 s: fitted rate -3.36 +/- 0.35 /s against the true -6.14 (and -6.08 +/- 0.05 for the same ring at the record start), about 45 percent too small in magnitude. *Effect on D8:* the sign was right and the 2-SE test unaffected in that case; the magnitude is biased toward zero, so a weakly damped ring from an abrupt onset can read as non-damped. This points the opposite way to DL-062, and the two do not cancel. Not tested on M3 output. In S5 ramps the onset should be gradual, so the bias is probably smaller there; that is reasoned, not tested. The 2/5/10 ms window shift (rate moves up to about 16 percent) is a sensitivity, not a bound on the bias.

**Proposed handling (not decided):**
1. 7b builds a seeded synthetic matrix: rings and growing oscillations with known rate from the S2 range (about -25 /s) through weak damping to zero and positive, step onset and smooth onset, noise-free and with additive noise, the same band and cycle rule. Report bias of the rate and, per method, the false "damped" rate at true rate 0 and the false "non-damped" rate at damped rates.
2. Candidate SE methods (each tested on the matrix): AR(1) effective sample size, Newey-West, one sample per cycle, block bootstrap. Candidate onset fixes are DL-073 (a) to (e); (a) start the fit a stated number of filter lengths after the crossing and (c) fit the analytic signal of the unfiltered v are the two I would test first, with (e) calibration on step-onset rings as the check.
3. The acceptance numbers (largest tolerated false-damped and false-non-damped rate; largest tolerated rate bias) are yours to set; I propose them in 7b from the matrix and mark them ours, UNVERIFIED, before looking at class results.
4. Any change of the fit start or of the signal changes the D8 wording of Table 2.1 ("fitted from the threshold crossing over the first 10 cycles").
